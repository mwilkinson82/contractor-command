# Job cost foundation — staged review and role model

This is **not a released Athena feature**. The SQL is a non-migration draft and is exercised only against synthetic PGlite and isolated PostgreSQL fixtures. The local source registers six guarded conversational tools, but code and its eventual coordinated migration must be released together. No live database, real customer data, payment transfer, or attachment storage has been used.

## Owner-approved role boundary

The owner approved the following model for this workflow. It is implemented only in the staged SQL; no live grants or policies have changed.

- An assigned superintendent may submit a **new** company-paid or personally reimbursable field expense for that assigned job and may read their own original submission. The superintendent cannot read the financial ledger, other submitters' entries, subsequent accounting versions, or payments through this path. A separate staged receipt workflow binds each upload and receipt to that superintendent's own submission and checks membership and job assignment at upload, registration, and read. A reimbursement payee is recorded as supplied and must be reviewed before payment; the submitter can see only their own submissions. No PO or subcontract is required. Receipt access does not grant direct access to the financial storage bucket or the billing document ledger.
- An assigned PM may review their own job's allocation and approve its payment basis for a specific immutable bill or reimbursement version. The PM does not post accounting or record a payment. PMs state whether work was verified, delivery is needed, or the payment is an advance/deposit; advance/deposit needs an explicit note. Nothing is inferred. A split requires approval for every affected job; an owner can document an explicit exception for each job. Substantive correction creates a new version and invalidates earlier PM approvals.
- Owner and AP, with full access to all affected jobs, may record accounting approval, posting, settlement evidence and evidence corrections. Accounting posting is distinct from PM payment approval. No settlement is recorded on a supplier bill or reimbursement without job-specific PM/owner coverage. Company-paid expenses create no reimbursement payable. No funds are moved. Existing full-scope access for all-access and assigned PM is unchanged, but it does not grant them accounting actions.
- Owner approval of company-wide PO terms belongs to the separately staged supplier/PO workflow; this cost slice does not modify it.

The same authorized actor can perform more than one permitted step; separation of duties is not currently required. The approving PM or an owner can withdraw an active approval with a reason. Withdrawal blocks future recorded payments until the job is approved again, but preserves prior payment evidence and does not imply a refund. A direct superintendent correction workflow is not yet implemented.

## Integrity and integration boundaries

Each write uses a database-created, actor/job/purpose/payload-bound, expiring review ticket and a confirmation assertion. A ticket is consumed transactionally, while an exact successful retry retains its request ID. Cross-actor, changed-payload, wrong-purpose, expired, and unconfirmed requests are rejected. The database can verify that a review ticket was created and the caller asserted confirmation; it cannot independently prove that the human actually saw or approved the review. That host trust boundary remains a security review item. Membership and applicable job scopes are checked again on write.

The database keeps append-only cost versions, conservative duplicate references, integer-cents allocation conservation and separate payment evidence. Corrections leave the earlier posted version effective until a successor is approved and posted. A corrected version requires fresh PM approvals before settlement. A whole-entry correction of erroneous settlement evidence requires a reason and retains history; it does not assert a bank reversal or refund. Shared settlement is tested for allocation conservation and access, but remains **denied by default** until a trusted common-payee identity is integrated. Partial corrections, actual refunds and shared-payment/correction chat paths are not implemented.

Draft PR #352 at head `9b5df5c54ebab9e41773321cd65a098f607c7ced` proposes project-linked `mcp_project_parties` and `mcp_purchase_orders`, plus immutable `mcp_purchase_order_versions` keyed by `(po_id,version)` and carrying a party snapshot. Synthetic tests use the actual staged PO schema and check exact company, job, matching party, snapshot and existing PO revision. Chat carries explicitly identified party, PO and revision through review and save. Linked costs may allocate only to that project in this draft. The lookup fails closed if supplier tables are absent. Coordinated integration must add durable referential integrity and resolve PO changes/deletion. A PO is a commitment, never evidence of cost or payment.

The duplicate check uses normalized counterparty name and document reference. Aliases or absent references can evade it, while same-name parties can collide. A document reference is metadata, not a stored original. A separately registered field receipt can be stored after the initial submission; supplier-bill originals are not integrated. Supplier identity reconciliation remains a prerequisite.

The scoped `mcp_cost_submission_read(job_id)` returns `posted_version` and `posting_events` with `version`, `posted_by`, and `posted_at`; each version contains its own `recorded_at`, reason, and `facts.allocations` by job/cost code. `posted_at` is the database-recorded posting instant; no independent accounting effective date was supplied (`accounting_effective_date_available: false`). `payments` are whole-record evidence with recorded dates and corrections, and `settlement_allocation_available: false` explicitly prevents inventing per-job payment allocation. Only users with full access to every job in every version can read the full record.
